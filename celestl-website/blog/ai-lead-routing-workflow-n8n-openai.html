How I built an AI lead-routing workflow with n8n and OpenAI

The full build, step by step: what worked, what broke, and what I would do differently.

Komal Siddharth
Founder, Celestl · 4 min read

Most service businesses I talk to don't have a lead problem. They have a sorting problem. Enquiries come in all day, and someone has to read each one, decide if it matters, pass it on and log it. That someone is usually the person who should be closing deals.

This is how I replaced that routine with a workflow that runs on its own, and the mistake I made in the first version.

The problem in one line

Good leads were waiting hours for a reply because a human had to touch every message before anything happened.

The workflow, step by step

  1. Catch every lead in one place. Website form, email and messages all feed into a single n8n workflow, so nothing depends on someone checking an inbox.
  2. Let the model read it. OpenAI pulls out the basics from each message: who it is, what they need, how urgent it sounds and whether a budget is mentioned.
  3. Tag with rules, not vibes. I wrote down what the team already meant by a hot lead and gave the model those rules with real past examples. The tags became consistent overnight.
  4. Route and notify. Each lead goes to the right person with a short summary, so they can reply without opening five tabs.
  5. Write it to the CRM. The record is created or updated automatically, with the original message attached.
The goal was never to replace the team. It was to make sure the first thing they touch is a conversation, not a spreadsheet.

What broke in the first version

The first version was too confident. It tagged every lead, even the vague ones, and a couple of half-written enquiries were marked as hot. The fix was simple: I gave the model permission to say it wasn't sure. Unclear leads now go to a person with a note explaining why.

That one change did more for the team's trust in the system than anything else I built.

What I would tell a founder

Start by writing down how your team decides things today. If you can explain your rules to a new hire, you can explain them to a workflow. The technology is the easy part. The clear rules are the real work.
